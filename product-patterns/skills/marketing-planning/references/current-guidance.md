# Marketing Planning

## Use When

Prepare an audience/positioning brief or a campaign/content/channel plan. For
live funnel instrumentation, variants or allocation, use the existing
growth-conversion-optimization owner; do not introduce a second optimizer.

## Inspect First

Read the product brief, verified capabilities, brand constraints and available
audience/channel evidence. Keep customer lists, account access and actual budget
policy in the target project. Separate user needs from assumed segments.

## Decision Rule

1. State the audience, problem, desired action and product evidence supporting
   the offer. If audience knowledge is missing, propose a segment as a hypothesis
   and a way to learn; do not manufacture a persona or demand evidence.
2. Draft a positioning/message brief: audience, value, reason to believe,
   scope limits, message options and call to action. Link factual claims to the
   supplied evidence; distinguish targets, estimates and observations.
   Check the actual headline, CTA and body, not only the plan's proof section.
   Speed, availability and who may use a feature are claims too: a capability
   name does not prove "in seconds", "anytime" or access for every role.
   Remove or narrow unsupported wording; caveats elsewhere do not repair it.
3. Select one small experiment suited to the requested learning. Define its
   cohort/time window, denominator, primary outcome, guardrail, observation
   limit or stop/review date, and decision rule. Proposed sample or budget limits
   are not approvals. Statistical claims need an appropriate design and evidence.
   State collection/eligibility rules or mark execution blocked until they are
   fixed. Use an observable decision rule; "materially better" with no defined
   comparison is not executable. A descriptive learning experiment can report
   counts and gaps without choosing a causal winner or inventing a threshold.
4. For supplied counts, state the exact numerator/denominator and collection
   limits. Do not infer unique people, human traffic, channel attribution,
   causality or uplift from unqualified event counts or one small cohort.
   Event counts also do not prove that a feature, payment path or prototype works;
   use separately supplied capability and execution evidence for those claims.
5. Hand the brief to writing/design for draft assets. Load
   `common/skills/human-authored-writing/SKILL.md` when writing audience-facing
   copy; load the existing CRO skill only when funnel work is actually needed.
   Execution uses existing scoped authorization; the brief grants none.

## Output

Produce a campaign brief: goal, audience hypothesis/evidence, message and proof,
channel rationale, draft asset needs, experiment definition, assumptions and
open decisions. Use the shared artifact-handoff contract for downstream work.
If only a message draft was requested, do not force a campaign or paid experiment.

## Common Rationalizations

- "We need a strong proof point." Use verified behavior or a labeled hypothesis;
  never turn an aspiration into a customer result.
- "Publishing is part of planning." Keep planning and externally visible
  execution distinct and reuse only matching existing authority.

## Red Flags

Raw events are described as qualified human conversions; a tiny cohort is called
significant; synthetic quotes become testimonials; a draft implies an approved ad.

## Do Not

Do not invent claims, benchmarks, testimonials, competitor facts or consent.
Do not add tools, trackers, paid model calls or outreach because a plan names them.

## Stop If

The requested factual claim cannot be supported, or the next external action
lacks authority. Flag that part; still deliver useful draft content where possible.

## Verification

Check every proof point, any rate calculation and its population/time window.
Verify an executable experiment definition without implying it was executed.
Ensure writing/design receives the same brief version, claim limits and gaps.
When product policy changes, update affected copy in a new version now, including
permission-sensitive invitations to act. Do not review only the unchanged feature
or defer correcting a known contradiction until publication. Apply the shared
artifact-handoff checks before passing the revised brief to its next owner.

## Report

Report the brief/version, evidence, message hypothesis, experiment, missing inputs
and next owner. Distinguish drafted assets, approved execution and actual results.
